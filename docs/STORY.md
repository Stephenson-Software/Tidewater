# The story, plainly

**Spoilers for the whole game.** In the game this is told in pieces — by villagers, in a ledger, on gravestones, at the endings — and the journal's "What is happening to you" page assembles the pieces you have found. This is the assembled version.

## Where you are

A fishing village. You came in on the coast road with a rod and no work, and slept on the docks. It is the ninth of the month, and when somebody said so at the tavern the room went quiet.

## What is happening to you

At eleven at night a bell rings, and you wake on the docks at eight in the morning of the same day. Nobody else notices. Nothing you do lasts — not money, not what you carry, not where you were standing. What you have **learned** lasts. That is the only thing that does, and it is how you get out.

## What happened here

Thirty years ago tonight a fishing boat called the *Marigold* went down in a storm off the point. Two people were lost: Harry Blythe, the mate, and Nell Reade, deckhand — Old Tom's daughter. Tom was her captain, and he came home.

When a boat is out in weather, this village does two things to bring her home: it **rings the harbour bell**, so she can hear where the harbour mouth is, and it **keeps the lighthouse lit**, so she can see the point. Every boat the village ever lost was called in and lit in, and the loss written in the bank's ledger as done. That night, neither was done.

- **The bell did not ring because it had no rope.** Tom had cut it off the bell himself, weeks earlier: his mooring line was rotten and old Gilbert at the shop would not sell him rope on credit, so he took the bell's and tied his boat up with it. It was coiled on the *Marigold*'s deck the night she went out. So when the storm came there was nothing on the bell to pull.
- **The lighthouse was dark.** The same shopkeeper had cut off Ada's lamp oil that week over an unpaid bill. Ada swung the lamp until the oil ran out at nine.

The *Marigold* could not hear the harbour and could not see the point. She struck it. Tom came ashore with the rope and has kept it in his cellar for thirty years. He does not speak of it. Ada watched from the lamp room and has never bought her oil from that shop since. The ledger says only "Rope found cut."

## Why the day repeats

A night that was never finished does not end. The village owed the *Marigold* a bell and a light and gave her neither, and for thirty years nobody said so. So the night of the ninth comes back round, on its anniversary, to whoever will finish it. This year that is you — the one person in the village who was not here, and so the one person who can look at it.

## The ways out

- **The bell.** Learn the *Marigold*'s name from Margaret's ledger; Old Tom, if you know the name, gives you the rope; hang it in the tower before the storm and ring it at eleven. Tom climbs the tower and tells you everything. Half of what the night was owed — enough to let the day end.
- **The light.** Learn from Ada what she saw and why the lamp went out; Gilbert's son sells you oil (and has an opinion about it); fill and light the lamp before nine. It holds through the storm, and Ada tells you everything. Half — enough to let the day end.
- **Both, in one day.** Light the lamp and hang the rope. The light holds at nine, the beam lights the front so you can reach the tower through the storm, and the bell rings at eleven with the light on the water. Tom says it all aloud. The night is finished, and will not come back.

After any ending the days are ordinary. After a half-ending the journal tells you which half is still owed.
